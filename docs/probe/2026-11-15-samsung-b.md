## Diagnosis

1. **The backoff fix worked.** Run 1's armed time (17.7%) was destroyed by the OS exponential backoff after each ~10-minute TIMEOUT; v0.0.2 returns `false` from `onStopJob` and schedules a fresh job itself. In this run the restart gaps are p50 29 ms / p95 239 ms (max 11.1 s), and armed time rose from 17.7% to **73.6%**. The TIMEOUT loop is no longer the problem.

2. **The remaining loss is the arming delay after plug-in, and it is large.** The job takes ~22 to ~32 min to start after power returns, and quick replugs never arm it (the ~20-replug M2 burst armed nothing). During each of those windows the job is unarmed, so (a) armed time cannot reach 90%, and (b) a cut that lands soon after a plug-in is invisible to the job. This is the same delay seen on the Android 15 emulator (ADR 0013) and in run 1, so it is not a one-off. Android documents that a charging-constraint job is deferrable and "not quite the same as simply being connected to power" (https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/job/JobInfo.Builder#setRequiresCharging(boolean)); it is scheduled at the system's convenience, not at plug-in. An expedited job cannot carry the charging constraint (M8), so that escape is closed.

3. **The receiver path is reliable.** The reference logger (a foreground service with a dynamic `ACTION_POWER_CONNECTED` / `ACTION_POWER_DISCONNECTED` receiver, i.e. the Station-mode mechanism of ADR 0004) saw every plug change within about 1 s (M7: 28 of ~20 plug changes settled within 1 s; 0 of 35 stop-time re-reads differed), and all five armed-unplugs were seen in 0.3 to 6.5 s. Receiver-driven observation does not have the job's arming delay.

4. **No reboots; one app restart** (handled as above); **one sticky/BatteryManager mismatch** of 35 (transient, resolved by the 1 s re-read).

## What this run shows and does NOT show

Shown (from the exported log, analysed on Node/JVM): the v0.0.2 immediate-reschedule fix removed the backoff loss (armed 17.7% -> 73.6%); the job's arming delay after plug-in (~22 to ~32 min) is the dominant remaining loss and fails both the 90% armed gate and the 60 s M2 gate; the foreground-service receiver path saw every plug change within ~1 s.

NOT shown: this run still did not reach 12 h of charging, so the gate is formally INCOMPLETE; a cut in Doze with the screen off over a long idle period is under-sampled; whether a **normal-mode** static manifest receiver (no foreground service, no persistent notification) arms as promptly as the foreground reference receiver is **not** measured here (the reference logger is a foreground service). That last question is the subject of the decision in ADR 0004 and would need a coordinated future probe (see the decision note).

Conclusion: fixing the backoff was necessary and moved armed time from 17.7% to 73.6%, but it is not sufficient. The job's long arming delay after plug-in keeps it below 90% and makes it unfit as the primary, prompt sensor on this phone. The decision follows in ADR 0001 (addendum) and ADR 0004 (addendum): receiver-driven arming becomes the primary observer; the job stays as a backup.
